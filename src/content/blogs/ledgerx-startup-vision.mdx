LedgerX is my ongoing startup project: an enterprise resource planning platform that aims to connect the everyday work of a business in one place. The idea grew out of my interest in ERP systems and the experience of building College ERP. Academic workflows taught me to think beyond individual screens and consider how different roles share information.

## The problem I want to solve

Imagine a small distributor that keeps its sales in spreadsheets, stock in a separate tool and payment records in accounting software. A sale can create several disconnected records. When those records disagree, someone has to investigate manually. LedgerX is intended to reduce that friction through connected business workflows, not simply add another dashboard.

The product vision covers accounting, purchasing, inventory, sales, GST, payroll, CRM and reporting. The longer-term ambition is to connect these capabilities with automation and AI-assisted decisions. The primary audience I want to explore is small and growing businesses that need more structure without immediately adopting a complicated enterprise suite.

## What an MVP should prove

A useful first release should support a complete commercial cycle: create a customer, register a product, place a sales order, issue an invoice, record a payment and reconcile inventory changes. Every action must respect organization boundaries and user permissions. Getting that cycle right matters more than displaying a large catalogue of unfinished modules.

## How I want to validate the idea

Before scaling the product, I want to understand where business users repeat manual work, what data they already maintain and which workflows they would trust a new application to handle. A pilot should reveal whether the software makes those tasks easier. Success could be evaluated through time to complete a workflow, reconciliation accuracy and repeat usage rather than just the number of features.

## Current status

LedgerX is in active development. The product vision is broader than the currently verified implementation; this series documents design priorities and plans, not a claim of commercial launch, paying customers or funding. My next focus is making the core workflows reliable enough for meaningful feedback.
